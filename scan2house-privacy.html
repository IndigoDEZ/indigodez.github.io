1. What the app does

Scan2House 3D: Block Builder turns a walk around a house into a block model for block-building games. You walk around the house while the app records with your phone's camera and with Google Play Services for AR (ARCore). Your phone then builds a block model from the recording, in about a minute. On a review screen you correct what the app got wrong: the corners, the wall heights, the roof, doors and windows, porches, decks and trees. Then the app exports game files, which you can hand to Minecraft or to any app you choose. All of this runs on your phone. The app has no internet access at all. The notice you accept at first launch and the About screen both link to this policy.

2. What the app records and keeps on your phone

Each scan is one folder in the app's own storage area on your phone. Recording writes these into it:

  • Photos of the house. Camera frames of 1920 by 1080 pixels, saved each time the phone has moved far enough while you record. A full walk around a house gives 1 to 2 GB of photos.
  • Depth maps. ARCore's depth estimate for each photo, with its confidence for each pixel, when your phone supports depth and the Depth switch is on.
  • Camera poses. Where the phone was and which way it pointed for each photo, with the lens settings, exposure and timing. Together they trace the path you walked.
  • Motion readings. The accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, gravity and rotation sensors, logged while you record.
  • Feature points. A sparse cloud of points that ARCore tracked on the house and its surroundings, when the Points switch is on.
  • Doors and windows. A small model running on your phone finds doors, windows and garage doors in each photo and saves their outlines. It finds nothing else. It does not recognize people or faces.
  • Corner taps. The house corners you mark with the Corner button, as positions in the scan.
  • Scan details. The side you chose to scan ("Whole building", "Front side" and so on), the recording settings, how long the walk took, and your phone's make and model, its Android version and the app version.

Building adds to the same folder: the block model and the list of features found (the outline, wall heights, roof sections, doors and windows, porches, decks and trees), the corrections and notes you make on the review screen, the review page and the model viewer, the game files (.mcpack, .nbt and .schem), a preview image of the model, and the build log.

Outside the scan folders the app keeps three small things: a technical note about your phone's cameras (the lenses and image sizes they offer, written once), a flag that you accepted the first-launch notice, and a flag that the Corner hint was shown.

All of this stays in the app's own folder on your phone. The app opts out of Android's backup, so none of it is copied to Google's servers by a phone backup. Nothing is written to your photo library. A scan stays until you delete it in the app or uninstall the app (section 10).

3. What we do not collect

We receive nothing from the app. It has no internet permission, so it cannot send anything by itself. The only permission it asks for is the camera. It never asks for your location, contacts, microphone, photo library or files. There are no accounts and no sign-in. There are no ads, no purchases, no analytics and no crash reports, and the app does not use an advertising ID. The review screen and the model viewer are web pages stored inside the app and shown offline; they load nothing from the internet.

4. Google Play Services for AR

This application runs on Google Play Services for AR (ARCore), which is provided by Google and governed by the Google Privacy Policy.

The app needs Google Play Services for AR and cannot run without it. It uses ARCore on your phone for motion tracking (the camera poses) and for depth. It does not use ARCore's cloud features (Cloud Anchors or Geospatial), and it never sends camera images anywhere. Google Play Services for AR is a separate app from Google, installed from the Play Store, and Google's own policy covers what it does.

5. What leaves your phone, and only when you send it

Nothing leaves your phone unless you tap a button that sends something. Each send goes through Android's share sheet, where you pick the app that receives it. There are three kinds:

  • Game files. Bedrock hands the .mcpack to Minecraft when it is installed, and otherwise opens the share sheet. Java shares the .nbt and .schem files. The game files hold the block model: the position and kind of each block. They hold none of your photos.
  • Send scan. Packs everything the walk recorded into one zip file: the photos, the depth maps, the camera poses, the motion readings, the feature points, the doors and windows found, your Corner taps and the scan details, including your phone's make and model. It leaves out the build. A full scan is 1 to 2 GB. Testers use it to send us a scan that did not build well.
  • Send results. Packs a much smaller file, with no photos and no depth maps: the block model and its features, the corrections and notes you made on the review screen, the game files, the build log, the camera poses, the feature points, the motion readings, your Corner taps and the side label. The camera poses and feature points still describe the shape of the house and the path you walked.

Every send is a copy. Where it goes is your choice, and the app keeps no record of it. The packed file waits in the app's cache until the next pack replaces it.

6. What we do with scans and results you send us

If you send a scan or a results pack to z.farm.technologies@gmail.com, or share a link to one with us, we receive a copy of that data about your house. We use it only to find out why a build went wrong and to improve the app. We never use it to train anything, we never sell it, and we never share or publish it. We keep it only while it helps us improve the app, and we delete it as soon as you ask. Email the same address to have everything you sent deleted; we confirm within 30 days. The photos are of your house, so send a scan only if you are comfortable with us having them. A results pack holds no photos.

7. Why the data is processed

  • To record a scan and build the block model and game files you ask for, on your phone. This is needed to provide the app to you.
  • To look into a scan or results pack you chose to send us, and to improve the app from it. This rests on your consent, given by sending it; you withdraw it by asking us to delete what you sent.
  • To comply with the law.

8. Who receives data

We do not sell personal data, and we do not share it for advertising. The app itself sends nothing to anyone. When you export game files or send a scan or results, the app you pick in the share sheet receives them (Minecraft, Drive, Dropbox, your email app, or any other), under that app's own terms and privacy policy. Google Play Services for AR runs under Google's privacy policy. We receive data only when you send it to us. Nobody else receives data unless the law requires it.

10. Your choices and rights

Because we receive nothing from the app, you control your data directly on your phone:

  • Delete, on a scan's build screen, asks "Delete this scan?" and then removes the scan, its model and your corrections from your phone. Files you already sent or exported are not affected.
  • Uninstalling the app removes every scan and everything else the app keeps. Clearing the app's storage in Android's Settings does the same.
  • The Depth and Points switches on the capture screen decide whether depth maps and feature points are recorded. The Corner button is optional.
  • Your scans are in open formats in the app's folder. You can copy a scan to a computer over USB.

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete or export your personal data, to object to or restrict its processing, and to withdraw consent. For anything you sent us, contact us at z.farm.technologies@gmail.com; we reply within 30 days (45 days for requests under California law). If you are in the EU or UK, you may also complain to your data-protection authority.

11. Other people and neighbors in your scans

Scan only a house you own, or one whose owner has given you permission. The app asks you to confirm this at first launch and reminds you on the capture screen. Keep people out of your scans where you can: if someone walks through, let them pass before you record that wall. A scan may still catch a neighbor's property, parked cars, license plates or passers-by. The app does not blur faces or plates, and it does not recognize or identify anyone; it only finds doors and windows. Think about what is in a scan before you send it to anyone, including us. You are responsible for respecting other people's privacy and the local rules on photography.

12. Children

Scan2House 3D: Block Builder is for people aged 13 and over. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from anyone, children included. The app does not ask for an age. There are no accounts, no social features and no chat. Contact us with any questions.

13. Security

Everything the app keeps stays in its own folder on your phone. On Android 11 and later, other apps cannot read that folder. The app has no internet permission, so it cannot send data on its own, and we run no server that holds your data. Scans and results that testers send us are kept in ordinary email or cloud storage under our control and seen only by the people working on the app. If we ever learned of a problem affecting personal data, we would act promptly and inform the affected users and authorities where the law requires.
